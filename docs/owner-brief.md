# H-Tech — Owner's Brief

*From the owner. This is what I want; how to build it is your job.*

I'm opening an online store for computer parts and peripherals — monitors, keyboards, mice, printers, CPUs, RAM, motherboards, cases, that kind of thing. People here rebuild and upgrade machines constantly, and nobody local does this well online.

We start with about **1,500 products across 7 or 8 categories**, from two distributors. Both send me their catalogs as spreadsheets, and their prices change — sometimes weekly, sometimes overnight when exchange rates move. Within the first year I want to be onboarding more distributors; I'd expect the catalog to reach **15–20 thousand products**. Customers must always see whether a part is actually in stock — nothing kills trust like ordering RAM that doesn't exist. And the price they see when they hit "order" is the price they pay, full stop, even if the distributor changed it an hour ago.

At the beginning I expect a **few hundred visitors a day**, mostly browsing and comparing. Buyers are a small fraction of that. But when we run promotions — back-to-school, end-of-year, a good deal on GPUs — traffic will spike hard for a few days; I've seen competitors get **ten times their normal traffic** in those windows, and their sites crawl. Mine shouldn't. Browsing has to *feel instant* — these customers compare five products before picking one, and if pages drag, they're gone.

Payment reality: this market runs on **cash on delivery**. Card payments online are something I want *eventually*, but at launch, customers place an order, we confirm it by phone or message, and the courier collects cash. Which means an order that's placed must **never, ever be lost** — a lost order isn't a bug to me, it's money and reputation gone. Orders go through states: placed, confirmed, handed to courier, delivered — and sometimes refused at the door, which happens with COD; the part goes back into stock.

Returns: electronics arrive dead sometimes. Customers get **7 days** to report a dead-on-arrival part; we swap or refund. I need to see in the admin which orders have open return cases.

Team: it's me plus **one assistant** managing catalog, prices, and orders through an admin screen. Neither of us is technical. During a promo we'll both be in there at once updating prices, so it can't fall over when two people edit things.

The store should basically **always be up** — but if you're telling me perfection costs a fortune, I care most that *ordering* works; if the reviews section hiccups for an hour I'll survive. Budget is small: this runs on modest hosting until revenue says otherwise. I'd rather it run lean and fast than fancy and fragile.

That's what I want. Tell me what you need from me, and tell me when I'm asking for something expensive so I can decide if it's worth it.
